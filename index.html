Quan Summit 2026-09-28: Defining Study Populations and Variables

In this workshop we’ll do a mini study looking at the effects of different comorbidities on Heart Failure (HF) readmission. This will involve,

  1. Selecting a HF cohort from the Discharge Abstract Database (DAD)
  2. Determining which patients were readmitted (our outcome)
  3. Identifying the comorbidities of interest in our cohort (our exposures), using the DAD and physician claims
  4. Running a simple survival analysis

Important

Construct episodes of care

A patient may have multiple entries in the DAD, but they could be considered one “Episode of Care”, e.g., a patient moved from a rural site to a larger hospital generates two DAD entries but should be considered one episode of care.

We are using a simple algorithm here, and considering any admission within 12 hours of discharge to be the same episode of care. CIHI uses a slightly different algorithm, with varying time windows depending on if the transfer was coded or not.

Determine the exposures for each patient

These will be the comorbidities that we want to check if they affect the outcome. Let’s pick a few.

Age

This will be the easiest variable to get, which we can get from the DAD. It can be computed from the date of admission and birthdate, but unless there is a clear need birthdate will likely not be released due to its identifiable nature. Instead the analyst will generally compute the age for you and release that.

Sex

Sex is a categorical variable, but there are a few things to keep in mind.

  1. The categories are not just M/F
  2. The data doesn’t do a very good job at disentangling gender and sex

COPD

This is our first comorbidity which we will look for in the DAD using the presented algorithms.

CKD

This is our second comorbidity which we will look for in the DAD using the presented algorithms.

Hypertension

This is the last comorbidity we will look at. As hypertension is not usually diagnosed in hospital, and is generally not directly responsible for a large use of resources in the hospital, it is known to have low sensitivity in the DAD. Therefore for this case we will also use claims data.
